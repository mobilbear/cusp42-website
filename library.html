What could go wrong, how badly, and how likely: the analyses behind CARI's questions and MoralWorld's catastrophes.

The founding argument that machine superintelligence could be the last invention humanity needs to make, or gets to make.

Human Compatible

Russell, 2019

Why AI built to pursue fixed objectives is dangerous, and a proposal for machines uncertain about human preferences.

The Alignment Problem

Christian, 2020

How machine-learning systems come to learn something other than what we intended.

Center for AI Safety, 2023

One sentence signed by hundreds of researchers: mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority.

An Overview of Catastrophic AI Risks

Hendrycks, Mazeika & Woodside, 2023

The scientific consensus, and its gaps, on the capabilities and risks of general-purpose AI.

AI, work and society

How AI reshapes jobs, incomes, institutions and power: the mechanisms in MoralWorld.

The Simple Macroeconomics of AI

Acemoglu, NBER, 2024

A task-based estimate that AI adds well under 1% to productivity over a decade.

Gen-AI: Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Work

Cazzaniga et al., IMF Staff Discussion Note, 2024

About 60% of jobs exposed in advanced economies, 40% in emerging and 26% in low-income ones.

Power and Progress

Acemoglu & Johnson, 2023

A thousand years of technology and who gets its gains: the choice is political, not automatic.

AI as Normal Technology

Narayanan & Kapoor, 2025

Impact comes through slow diffusion and institutional change, not capability alone.

The Intelligence Curse

Drago & Laine, 2025

When intelligence is abundant, powerful actors lose their incentive to invest in people.

Too Much Intelligence, Not Enough Society: Why the Old Order Cannot Absorb AI

SSRN working paper, 2026

Why the binding constraint is society's capacity to absorb change, not the supply of intelligence.

The Great Decoupling: Frontier AI, the Broken Ladder, and the End of the Last Middle Class

SSRN working paper, 2026

How AI can raise output while breaking the entry-level ladder and hollowing out the middle class.
