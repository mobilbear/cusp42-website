Two questions

Risk and transformation, at the same depth.

Most attention goes either to the catastrophes or to the productivity. We think the transition has to be studied as one thing: the risks are part of how AI reshapes society, and the reshaping is where most of the risk lives.

The risk question

What could go wrong, how likely is it, and when?

Loss of control over critical systems. Misuse that lets a few do enormous harm. Accidents in AI-run grids, markets and food systems. AI-accelerated escalation between powers. The slow collapse of shared truth. We track the drivers, and we simulate the odds.

The human question

What happens to us when intelligence becomes abundant?

Work, careers and the ladders into the middle class. Institutions built for slower change. Meaning, agency and attention. What it means to be needed, and what remains distinctively human. We explore it with simulation, structured debate and thought experiments.

What we are building

What a simulation shows

One future is an anecdote. The distribution is the result.

On today's AI trend, roughly one simulated future in three meets a global AI catastrophe before 2050. Most of the others are not catastrophes but deep, uneven change: broken career ladders, strained public finances, eroded trust, and in some, real shared prosperity. Which world we get depends on choices that are still open.

How we work

Serious about uncertainty.

A thought lab earns trust by showing its working. These are the rules we hold ourselves to.

Scenarios, not forecasts

We show what stated mechanisms imply under stated assumptions. Change the assumptions and the future changes: that is the point.

Odds over anecdotes

One simulated history is one draw of luck. We report how often outcomes happen across many.

Assumptions in the open

Every index question has its anchors; every simulated change traces to a mechanism and a data source.

Neutral by design

One set of rules for every country; no regime labels; no country blamed for a war on our posters.
